Onboarding conversion: self-serve onboarding 2.0

  • Owned analytics for the redesign of self-serve onboarding. One long form became guided steps (store, business, financial) with in-page validation and AI document extraction and blur detection, so errors are caught before submission rather than rejected after it.
  • Defined the metrics the redesign was judged on (adoption, conversion, rejection, and onboarding SLA) and built the dashboard covering ~50k new merchants a month for operations in all 8 countries, with a stuck-merchant list for follow-up.

Result: self-serve adoption grew ~25pp regionally, onboarding conversion rose ~8pp, and the rejection rate fell by almost half. This freed operations capacity from manual onboarding for fraud and risk verification.

Activation: new merchants transacting within 30 days

  • Investigate why a new merchant did or did not get a first transaction within 30 days, with a random forest model using SHAP value. The top 5 drivers (item selection, item photo coverage, go-live timing, early cancellations, and campaign adoption) became the recommendations in new-merchant guidance, shown in priority order on the merchant app homepage.
  • Set item-count and photo-coverage thresholds per country and category from the medians of merchants who reached a first transaction.
  • Compared auto go-live defaults across countries (3, 7, 14, or 30 days). Auto-launched merchants transacted at a far lower rate than merchants who chose to go live. Proposed removing auto go-live, because it drove many new-merchant cancellations, and identified 7–10 days as the best go-live window.
  • Found that 9% of new merchants received orders but never fulfilled them, mostly because the store was not open yet. This led to an opening-hours review and a go-live readiness step in new-merchant guidance.

Result: a 1-month A/B test showed +1.3pp on the 7-day transacting rate. After 100% rollout, the 30-day KPI rose +7pp within 3 months, with better item selection, fewer new-merchant cancellations, and higher campaign adoption.

Merchant quality: Top Service Program

  • Supported the design of a tiered quality program (Top, Good, Basic) for transacting merchants. Thresholds covered store online rate, fulfillment rate, order delay, cancellation, incident rate, and item description and photo coverage. Higher tiers earn incentives. Poor performers lose access to co-funded subsidy programs.

Result: the share of merchants in the Top and Good tiers increased by 2pp, cancellation and incident rates declined, and more orders were delivered on time. That supported the decision to roll the program out to all merchants.
